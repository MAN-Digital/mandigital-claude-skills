# Worked Example: Revenue Context Webinar Campaign

This case demonstrates how research, creative direction, human selection, character continuity, and controlled image production became a coherent campaign. Use it as a process reference. Do not reuse its character, scenes, headlines, or layout for an unrelated brief.

The runnable example config is `../assets/examples/revenue-context/revenue-context.campaign.toml`. Its visual references and approved outputs live beside it.

## 1. Brief and fact lock

The assignment was to promote MAN Digital's Revenue Context webinar on 22 September at 15:00 CEST. The audience included RevOps, finance, sales operations, and go-to-market leaders dealing with the gap between a signed deal and reliable revenue reporting.

The initial factual core was kept narrow:

- event: Revenue Context webinar;
- date and time: 22 September, 15:00 CEST;
- business problem: important contract context is often lost between sales, operations, finance, and reporting;
- desired action: register;
- proof and claims: no invented performance statistics.

The production gate remains pending in the example config because generating approved files is not the same as authorizing external publication.

## 2. Research journey

Research used two primary workstreams and expanded them into 63 reviewed results:

1. **Operational truth:** practitioner language around what was sold, contract terms, handoffs, recurring revenue, MRR changes, upgrades, downgrades, churn, and manual reconciliation.
2. **Creative precedent:** B2B and adjacent-category advertising that made operational pain human, contextual, and visually immediate.

Representative sources included:

- Jeff Ignacio, “Do You Really Know What You Sold?” — language around the post-sale gap between the commercial promise and operational understanding: <https://revengine.substack.com/p/do-you-really-know-what-you-sold>
- HubSpot Community discussion of recurring-revenue deal handling — evidence of complex operational workarounds rather than a campaign visual template: <https://community.hubspot.com/t5/Tips-Tricks-Best-Practices/Recurring-Revenue-Deals-Upgrades-Downgrades-Churns-Renewals-and/m-p/1177154>
- Little Black Book coverage of Intuit Enterprise Suite — a reference for relatable human frustration, contextual staging, and a campaign system with many scenes: <https://lbbonline.com/news/Intuitions-Relatable-Take-on-ERP-Advertising>
- Capture Expense brand work — a reference for confident B2B brand building and situational out-of-home thinking: <https://marcommnews.com/b2b-brand-capture-expense-invests-in-first-brand-building-ooh-work-with-insiders/>

The useful principles extracted from reference work were:

- show the cost of an abstract systems problem through a recognizable human moment;
- let the location and props carry part of the message;
- use direct, blunt copy rather than explaining a platform feature list;
- create a family of situations, not one key visual resized repeatedly;
- vary camera angles and office zones so the campaign feels observed rather than templated.

The protected expressions—specific Intuit lines, cast, wardrobe, compositions, layouts, branding, and scene choreography—were explicitly excluded.

## 3. Tension synthesis

Repeated observations converged into a human tension:

> The CRM can say “closed-won” while the people responsible for revenue still have to reconstruct what was actually promised.

The emotional consequence was not generic software frustration. It was the quiet absurdity of being surrounded by systems and dashboards while the contractual truth remained manual, scattered, or ambiguous.

This led to three territories:

### A. Revenue Archaeology

Teams excavate emails, documents, and notes to reconstruct contract history. Strong visual metaphor, but it risked becoming prop-heavy and overly theatrical.

### B. Closed-Won Hangover

The moment after celebration reveals the operational mess. Emotionally sharp and memorable, but a pure hangover metaphor could overstate or trivialize the audience's work.

### C. The Human Revenue API

One person becomes the unofficial integration layer among CRM, contracts, approvals, finance, and reporting. This was most specific to the audience and most naturally supported a recurring character.

The human creative decision selected **The Human Revenue API**, while retaining the blunt recognition energy of **Closed-Won Hangover**.

## 4. Campaign platform

The working campaign line was:

> Closed-won is not revenue visibility.

The campaign moves through a three-beat story:

1. **Recognition:** show an absurd but credible post-signature office moment.
2. **Diagnosis:** name the missing context, manual reconstruction, or contract ambiguity.
3. **Resolution:** position the webinar as the place to understand how revenue context should flow.

The role of MAN Digital is not to mock the worker. It is to recognize the invisible work and offer a better operating model.

## 5. Campaign world

### Invariants

- the same woman appears throughout as the human integration layer;
- dark curly hair in a loose, messy bun and natural styling;
- dry exhaustion, quiet disbelief, or matter-of-fact frustration;
- real office locations with evidence of fragmented processes;
- direct on-camera flash, crisp shadows, imperfect documentary texture;
- high-contrast MAN Digital blue, white display type, and orange action accent;
- large copy suitable for feed viewing;
- one dominant idea per asset.

### Variables

- lounge, kitchen, meeting room, copier or print zone;
- eye-level, overhead, wide environmental, and compressed corner angles;
- leaning, rebuilding a spreadsheet, explaining a contract, or handling paper evidence;
- contract pages, printouts, cables, coffee, laptop, dashboards, and whiteboards;
- copy that names a different failure mode;
- 3:4 master compositions and separately recomposed 1:1 versions.

### What was avoided

- glossy stock-office polish;
- generic SaaS gradients and floating UI cards;
- tiny event information;
- treating the character as incompetent;
- copying the reference campaign's typographic arrangement;
- random mess that did not support a revenue-operations story.

## 6. Scene system

| Sequence | Scene | Camera and location | Recognition moment | Message role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Lounge | raw, close environmental portrait | context disappeared after the deal moved forward | establish the platform |
| 02 | Kitchen | different office zone and working angle | MRR is being rebuilt manually | show recurring-revenue reconstruction |
| 03 | Meeting room | presentation or explanation angle | nobody can answer what is actually contracted | expose contract ambiguity |
| 04 | Copier zone | wider contextual frame with paper evidence | another dashboard cannot repair the record | challenge the default technology response |

The campaign could extend into approvals, renewals, product entitlements, billing exceptions, and handoff notes, provided each new scene reveals a distinct consequence and preserves the same platform.

## 7. Production method

The 3:4 execution was treated as the master because it allowed a strong character portrait and enough environmental evidence. The 1:1 work was recomposed rather than mechanically cropped.

Each image was generated or edited as a separate scene with:

- the identity reference attached;
- stable character invariants stated explicitly;
- one office location and one human action;
- a defined camera angle and flash treatment;
- space reserved for exact typography and the approved MAN Digital logo;
- exclusions for accidental text, extra marks, and irrelevant decor.

A notable QA repair removed an unintended background poster. The approved composition was preserved and only the defect was corrected. This is the preferred response when a successful image has a local artifact.

## 9. Why the campaign works as a teaching example

The campaign did not begin with “make a raw photograph.” The visual treatment emerged from a sequence:

```text
operational evidence
  -> human tension
  -> multiple territories
  -> human selection
  -> campaign platform
  -> recurring-character world
  -> scene matrix
  -> master prototypes
  -> targeted QA repairs
  -> recomposed format variants
```

Its most reusable lesson is that research does not choose the ad. Research supplies true material; creative direction transforms that material into a surprising organizing idea; art direction creates an ownable world; audience feedback pressure-tests clarity; and production protects the system execution by execution.

## 10. Extension rules

When extending this exact campaign:

- keep the approved character identity and visual grammar;
- create a new operational consequence, not a duplicate pose;
- vary both office zone and camera logic;
- keep event facts prominent and verified;
- add the new scene to the matrix and production manifest;
- reopen prototype approval if the extension changes tone or platform behavior.

For a different campaign, return to discovery. Do not transplant this character or “messy office frustration” treatment simply because it worked here.
