# Creative Direction System

Use this reference after research synthesis and before production. The goal is to turn evidence into a repeatable campaign idea, not a collection of attractive ads.

## Convert evidence into a creative leap

Build this chain explicitly:

1. **Observed truth** — what people repeatedly say, do, avoid, or improvise.
2. **Human tension** — the emotional contradiction beneath the operational problem.
3. **Strategic proposition** — the useful belief the brand can credibly own.
4. **Creative leap** — the surprising metaphor, behavior, world, or device that makes the proposition visible.
5. **Campaign platform** — a concise organizing idea that can generate many executions.

Do not begin with an aesthetic. Aesthetic choices should dramatize the tension and make the platform recognizable.

Examples must differ by situation, framing, and message while clearly belonging to one platform. Three headlines on the same layout do not prove range.

## Territory evaluation

Score each territory from 1 to 5. Write the reason beside the number; arithmetic alone is not a decision.

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Strategic truth | Does it dramatize a verified audience tension? |
| Brand ownership | Is the connection to the offer credible and specific? |
| Originality | Is the leap meaningfully different from category convention and references? |
| Instant recognition | Can the audience feel the problem before reading supporting copy? |
| Campaign range | Can it sustain at least three distinct executions and several channels? |
| Cultural fit | Does it make sense in the target market and moment? |
| Craft potential | Can art direction make it visually unmistakable? |
| Production feasibility | Can it be produced consistently within time, budget, and rights constraints? |

Recommend a territory with reasoning, but leave the approval decision to the named human creative director. Record the rejected territories and why they were not chosen.

## Campaign bible

Once a territory is approved, define the campaign world before generating final assets.

### Strategic core

- single audience truth;
- single campaign platform;
- product or offer role;
- desired reaction and action;
- claims that are allowed, conditional, or prohibited.

### Invariants

Invariants make the series recognizable. Specify:

- recurring character, casting rule, or object;
- wardrobe, age range, demeanor, and identity anchors when applicable;
- photographic or illustrative grammar;
- palette and brand marks;
- headline architecture and tone;
- recurring motif, prop behavior, or compositional rule;
- content that must never appear.

### Variables

Variables prevent repetition. Deliberately vary:

- office zone or real-world location;
- camera height, distance, lens feel, and point of view;
- action, pose, and emotional beat;
- problem object or evidence of the pain;
- headline and product proof;
- channel composition and aspect ratio.

Keep character identity and campaign grammar stable while varying the scene. Avoid regenerating essentially the same composition with cosmetic changes.

## Character bible

For a recurring character, capture:

- identity reference asset;
- stable facial and physical characteristics;
- age presentation;
- hair, makeup, and wardrobe rules;
- emotional range;
- role in the story;
- what may vary between scenes;
- what must remain identical;
- exclusions that prevent accidental stereotyping or glamourization.

Use the character as a narrative anchor, not decorative continuity. Each appearance should reveal a new consequence, ritual, or reaction.

## Visual grammar

Describe the image system in production language:

- medium: photography, collage, illustration, 3D, typography, or a controlled hybrid;
- lighting: source, hardness, direction, color, and intentional imperfections;
- camera: framing, lens character, flash behavior, depth, movement, and crop;
- setting: texture, mess level, era cues, and realism;
- composition: focal hierarchy, negative space, logo zone, and headline zone;
- finish: grain, contrast, color cast, paper texture, or retouching limits.

Define both positive rules and exclusions. “Raw office flash” is incomplete unless it also says what raw means and which polished commercial cues to avoid.

## Copy system

Write a repeatable architecture rather than isolated lines.

```text
Recognition line -> names the lived moment
Diagnosis line   -> reveals why the moment happens
Resolution line  -> introduces the brand's role
Action line      -> gives one next step
```

Keep the hierarchy readable at channel viewing distance. The visual should carry one idea; copy should sharpen it rather than explain everything.

## Scene matrix

Plan the series before production:

| Scene | Location | Camera angle | Human beat | Problem evidence | Message | Brand role | Format risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Reject matrices where location, angle, action, and message barely change. A strong matrix creates narrative breadth while keeping the platform intact.

## Synthetic audience review

Use synthetic audiences only to reveal ambiguity, weak recognition, cultural risk, or likely objections. Preserve exact prompts and label findings as directional.

Do not ask synthetic audiences to choose the final creative direction. The human creative director interprets the feedback and owns the decision.

## Final territory gate

Before approval, answer:

- What verified truth does this dramatize?
- What is the creative leap in one sentence?
- What makes the campaign recognizable without the logo?
- What can vary across ten executions?
- What cannot vary without breaking the idea?
- Why is this native to the brand rather than merely entertaining?
- Which reference principles influenced it, and how is the execution distinct?
