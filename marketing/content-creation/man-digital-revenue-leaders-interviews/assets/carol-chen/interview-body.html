MAN Digital invited Carol Chen, VP Revenue Operations at Flywire, to discuss when a company should invest in Revenue Operations and how the function changes as the business scales.

Why we asked Carol

A decade working across the Revenue Operations remit

Carol describes roughly ten years in Revenue Operations, beginning in sales operations and expanding into go-to-market operations, insights and analytics, tools, enablement, and sales compensation.

The company

Flywire

In the interview, Carol explains that Flywire’s teams support four verticals and coordinate marketing strategy with the leaders responsible for each vertical. Revenue Operations helps translate that strategy into campaigns, execution, and measurement.

What you’ll learn

When to start RevOps, what to hire for first, and how the function changes with scale

Carol connects the first RevOps investment to CRM ownership, explains how data and sales judgement work together in forecasting, and shares practical guidance for agencies, IPO readiness, retention, and the tool stack.

How do you define revenue operations to someone who has not had it before?

Revenue Operations supports the complete revenue cycle rather than only the sales process. It looks at what happens before an opportunity exists, during the opportunity, and after a customer signs, so the business can understand marketing return, sales execution, retention, and the health of existing revenue together.

At what point should a company hire its first RevOps person?

Carol’s practical trigger is earlier than a formal department: once a company has a few sales representatives and a CRM instance that needs an owner. Without that ownership, the business may not capture the fields needed to understand win rates, losses, ideal customers, and future growth.

The shape of the team can grow gradually. A Salesforce administrator can initially sit elsewhere in the organisation before Revenue Operations becomes a separate function.

Start thinking about Revenue Operations once you have a few representatives and a CRM instance to manage.

Which capabilities should an early RevOps team build first?

Carol starts with a Salesforce administrator who can establish reliable CRM ownership. An analytics hire can follow to answer questions about required headcount, growth pace, run rate, and where the business is likely to finish.

The next capability depends on the organisation’s biggest gap. A team struggling with product demonstrations or negotiation may need enablement, while a young company may ask one strong operator to cover several responsibilities before building a larger specialist team.

What does a trustworthy revenue forecast combine?

Carol contrasts two useful perspectives. Revenue Operations can build a formula-driven view from CRM data, including deal counts, stage conversion, close dates, and performance by representative. Sales leaders add direct knowledge of individual deals and may adjust the view accordingly.

For leadership planning, Revenue Operations can connect the forecast to average quota, headcount, current run rate, and the growth target. The most useful view combines that data-backed model with the sales team’s deal context.

When should a company build RevOps internally, and when should it use an agency?

An agency can make sense when a smaller company needs only a few hours of specialist support each week, or when the team has a concentrated one-time project such as rebuilding Salesforce or implementing CPQ. Those projects need extra capacity for a defined period without creating the same ongoing workload afterwards.

For the continuing function, Carol generally favours internal ownership. Turnover on an external team can reopen knowledge gaps and force the company to spend time retraining new people. A practical hybrid is to keep ownership and company context internally, then bring in an agency for project spikes.

How does the RevOps mandate change as a company approaches an IPO?

Before an IPO, processes and rules can still be flexible. As the company prepares for the public market, Revenue Operations has to formalise validations, approval paths, discount controls, and opportunity-change governance.

The standard changes because the numbers must become clean, defensible, and auditable. Carol describes a meaningful ramp-up in process discipline before the IPO and a different stakeholder environment afterwards.

Why has retention become a bigger part of the RevOps agenda?

Existing customers are generally less expensive to retain than new customers are to acquire. If a company keeps winning new business while current accounts cancel, growth leaks out of the other end of the funnel.

Carol sees the shift from reactive account management to proactive customer success as part of this change. Revenue Operations therefore needs a full-cycle view that covers what happens before an opportunity, through the sale, and after the customer signs.

How should RevOps decide which tools deserve a place in the stack?

Start with usage and the problem the tool was meant to solve. If people are not using it, or it does not solve that problem, Carol’s view is that the company should remove it or replace it rather than preserve the spend.

Consolidation can also reduce the operational burden. Every additional tool brings security and legal review, implementation, training, adoption monitoring, and ongoing oversight. A smaller set of tools that covers more needs makes it easier to prove ROI and sustain adoption.

Key takeaways

— Revenue Operations supports the end-to-end revenue cycle, not only sales administration.

— A few representatives plus an unmanaged CRM can be enough to justify the first investment.

— Start with system ownership, then add analytics or enablement according to the business gap.

— Forecasting benefits from both formula-driven CRM analysis and sales leaders’ deal context.

— Keep ongoing RevOps knowledge internally and use agencies for limited capacity or defined projects.

— Retention, auditable processes, tool adoption, and demonstrable ROI become more important as the business matures.
